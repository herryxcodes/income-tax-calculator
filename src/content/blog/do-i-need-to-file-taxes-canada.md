You should file a tax return even if you earned nothing. A $0 return is perfectly valid, and skipping it means you're giving up free money from the government. Hundreds of dollars a year, sometimes thousands.

Let's break down who's legally required to file a 2026 return, who should file by choice, and exactly what you're leaving on the table if you don't.

## Who Must File a Tax Return

CRA requires you to file a 2026 return if any of the following apply:

- **You owe tax.** If your employer didn't deduct enough, or you have income from self-employment, investments, or rental properties that results in a balance owing, you must file.
- **CRA sends you a request to file.** They can do this even if you have no income. If they ask, you file.
- **You disposed of capital property.** Sold stocks, crypto, or real estate (including your home)? You must report it, even if you had a loss.
- **You have to repay Old Age Security (OAS) or Employment Insurance (EI) benefits.** The clawback is calculated on your return.
- **You want to claim the Canada Workers Benefit**, including advance payments. CRA needs your return to work out what you're entitled to.
- **You owe CPP contributions on self-employment income**, or you haven't finished repaying a Home Buyers' Plan or Lifelong Learning Plan withdrawal from your RRSP.

Miss the **April 30, 2027** deadline when you owe money and CRA charges a late-filing penalty of 5% of your balance owing plus 1% for each full month you're late, up to 12 months. That adds up fast.

## Who Should File (Even If You Don't Have To)

This is the bigger group, and it includes a lot of people who don't realize what they're missing.

One benefit you can cross off the list: the Canada Carbon Rebate. It ended with the April 2025 payment when the federal consumer fuel charge was removed, so it's no longer a reason to file. Everything below still is.

### Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit (Formerly the GST/HST Credit)

The GST/HST credit became the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit in July 2026, and the government raised it by 25% for five years. It's a quarterly payment for low- and modest-income Canadians. Based on your 2025 return, a single person can receive up to **$679** from July 2026 to June 2027, couples up to $890, plus $234 for each child under 19.

Your 2026 return sets your payments from July 2027 to June 2028. CRA hasn't published those amounts yet.

You get it automatically -- but only if you file a return. No return, no payments. CRA uses your reported income to calculate eligibility. If you don't file, they assume you don't exist.

### Canada Child Benefit (CCB)

If you have kids under 18, the CCB pays up to **$8,157 per child under 6** and **$6,883 per child aged 6-17** for the July 2026 to June 2027 payment period, which is based on your 2025 return. Your 2026 return determines payments from July 2027 to June 2028; CRA will publish those inflation-adjusted amounts closer to then. The amount phases out at higher incomes, but even middle-income families receive something.

Both parents (or the primary caregiver and their spouse) must file returns for the payments to continue. If either person skips filing, CCB payments stop.

### Canada Disability Benefit

If you're 18 to 64 and approved for the disability tax credit, the Canada Disability Benefit pays up to **$204.20 a month** from July 2026 to June 2027. It's income-tested, so you -- and your spouse or common-law partner, if you have one -- must file your federal returns to receive it.

### Provincial and Territorial Benefits

Every province has its own credits tied to your tax return:

- **Ontario:** Trillium Benefit (combines energy credit, property tax credit, and sales tax credit)
- **BC:** BC Family Benefit (the Climate Action Tax Credit ended with its final payment in April 2025)
- **Alberta:** Alberta Child and Family Benefit
- **Quebec:** Solidarity Tax Credit

These are real dollars, and the amounts depend on your province and situation. But they all require a filed return.

### Canada Workers Benefit (CWB)

If you earned more than $3,000 of working income in 2026 and your adjusted net income was under roughly $38,500, the CWB can put up to **$1,665** back in your pocket for 2026 (for single individuals; up to $2,869 for families). It starts shrinking once a single person's adjusted net income passes $27,392. It's specifically designed for low-income workers, and Alberta, Quebec, and Nunavut use their own versions.

You claim it on your return. If you don't file, you don't get it.

### RRSP Contribution Room

Here's one people forget. Your RRSP contribution room is calculated as 18% of your earned income from the previous year, up to the annual maximum. CRA calculates this from your filed return.

No return filed? No new contribution room added. You could be working, earning $50,000, and building zero RRSP room because CRA doesn't know about your income. That's a problem that compounds over years.

## Students: File Your Return

Even if you worked part-time and earned under the basic personal amount ($16,452 in 2026, meaning you owe zero federal tax), file anyway. Three reasons:

**RRSP room.** A summer job paying $8,000 gives you $1,440 in RRSP contribution room. That room carries forward forever. By the time you're 30 and earning a real salary, you could have $15,000-$20,000 of accumulated room ready to use.

**Tuition tax credits.** If you can't use your tuition credits this year (because you don't owe enough tax), they carry forward to future years when you do. But you have to file to put them on the books.

**Benefit payments.** If you're 19 or older, you can qualify for the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit. That's up to $679 a year (at July 2026 to June 2027 rates) just for filing a return that takes 15 minutes.

## Zero Income? File Anyway

"But I literally made $0."

Still file. A $0 return tells CRA your income is zero, which means you can qualify for the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit and provincial benefits, and your situation is properly documented. Some people skip filing for years and then wonder why they're not getting benefit payments. This is why.

Filing a $0 return is simple. Most tax software will walk you through it in under 10 minutes. There's nothing to calculate -- you're just confirming that your income was zero and collecting what you're owed.

## What Happens If You File Late but Don't Owe?

Good news. If CRA owes you money (a refund), there's no penalty for filing late. You won't pay a cent extra.

Bad news. Your benefit payments -- the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit, CCB, provincial credits -- won't flow until your return is processed. File two years late and you'll get a lump sum for missed payments, but you'll have gone two years without that money.

CRA can only go back 10 years for refunds. If you haven't filed in over a decade, anything older than 10 years is gone for good.

## The 2026 Filing Deadline

For the 2026 tax year, the general filing deadline is **April 30, 2027**. Self-employed individuals have until June 15, 2027 to file, but any taxes owed are still due April 30.
