If you file your 2026 return online, expect your refund in about two weeks. If you mail a paper return, you're looking at about twelve weeks. That's the short answer.

Now let's talk about what can speed things up, what can slow things down, and how to actually check where your money is.

## How Long Does CRA Take to Process Your Return?

The Canada Revenue Agency publishes service standards for processing individual tax returns. For returns filed on time:

- **Online (NETFILE or EFILE):** notice of assessment within 2 weeks of CRA receiving your return. CRA aims to meet this 95% of the time.
- **Paper filing:** notice of assessment within 12 weeks of CRA receiving your return. CRA aims to meet this 85% of the time. (The paper standard used to be 8 weeks; CRA lengthened it to 12 weeks in 2025-2026.)
- **Non-resident returns:** CRA says these take up to 16 weeks.

These are targets, not guarantees, and they only apply to returns received by the filing deadline. If you file online and use direct deposit, CRA says your refund can arrive in as little as eight business days. Paper filers should count from the date CRA receives the envelope, not from when they finished filling it out. CRA also posts current processing times on its "Check CRA processing times" page, updated monthly.

## How to Check Your Refund Status

Two ways to do it:

1. **CRA My Account** -- Sign in at canada.ca and use the progress tracker to see whether your return has been received, is being processed, or has been assessed. You can sign in from your phone's browser too.
2. **Automated phone line** -- Call 1-800-959-8281 and choose the automated refund option. Have your SIN and your return handy.

If you just filed yesterday, give it a few days before checking. CRA asks you to wait 12 weeks after filing (16 weeks if you live outside Canada) before calling an agent about your refund.

## Get Your Refund Faster: Use Direct Deposit

If you're still getting refund cheques in the mail, you're adding days to your wait. Direct deposit puts the money straight into your bank account the moment CRA releases it.

Set it up in CRA My Account under "Direct deposit." You can also do it through your bank. Set it up before you file if you haven't already.

## Why Your Refund Might Be Delayed

Filed three weeks ago and still nothing? A few common reasons:

**Missing or mismatched slips.** If the income on your return doesn't match what employers and banks reported to CRA, your return gets flagged for manual review. Double-check that you included all your T4s, T5s, and T3s.

**Math errors.** Tax software catches most of these, but if you filed on paper, a simple addition mistake can send your return to the back of the line.

**Selected for review.** CRA reviews a percentage of returns every year. If yours is selected, they may ask for receipts to support your claims. This can add weeks or even months.

**First-time filer.** If you've never filed a Canadian tax return before, CRA may take extra time to verify your identity and set up your account.

**Outstanding returns from previous years.** If you haven't filed your 2025 or 2024 return, CRA may hold your 2026 refund until those are sorted out.

**Money you owe the government.** CRA can keep all or part of your refund to cover a balance owing, unpaid student loans, EI or social assistance overpayments, or other federal and provincial debts.

## What If You Owe Money Instead?

No refund is coming if your assessment shows a balance owing. Your payment is due by **April 30, 2027**.

Can't pay the full amount? File your return anyway. The late-filing penalty (5% of what you owe plus 1% per full month, up to 12 months) is separate from any interest charges. Filing on time eliminates the penalty even if you can't pay right away.

CRA offers payment arrangements for people who can't pay in full. Call 1-888-863-8657 to set one up, or use the payment arrangement option in My Account. They'll work out a schedule based on what you can afford.

## CRA Will Never Text You a Refund Link

This needs to be said clearly: CRA will never email or text you a link to claim a refund, and it never asks for payment by gift card, prepaid credit card, or cryptocurrency. Refunds only arrive by direct deposit or a cheque in the mail. If someone contacts you claiming to be from CRA and asks for personal information or banking details, it's a scam. Hang up.

CRA's legitimate emails only tell you that new mail is waiting in My Account. They don't include account details or links asking for your information.

If you're unsure whether a communication is legitimate, log into My Account directly (don't click any links in emails) or call CRA's main line at 1-800-959-8281.

## After Your Refund: Notice of Assessment

Once CRA processes your return, you'll receive a Notice of Assessment (NOA). This is your official tax receipt for the year. It shows what CRA calculated for your tax, credits, and refund or balance owing.

Keep it. You'll need your NOA to confirm your RRSP contribution room for next year, and some lenders require it for mortgage applications.

If CRA changes anything on your return later -- say, after a review -- you'll get a Notice of Reassessment instead. Read it carefully. If you disagree with the changes, you can file a formal objection; for most individuals the deadline is 90 days after the date on the notice or one year after the filing deadline, whichever is later.

## File Early, Get Paid Early

The filing deadline for 2026 returns is **April 30, 2027**, but there's no reason to wait. CRA usually starts accepting returns online in the last full week of February and will announce the exact 2027 date early in the year (2025 returns opened on February 23, 2026). The earlier you file, the sooner your refund arrives.
