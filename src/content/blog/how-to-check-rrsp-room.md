There are three ways to find out your RRSP contribution room: check your CRA account online, look at your Notice of Assessment, or call CRA directly. Here's how each method works and what to do if the number doesn't match what you expected.

## Method 1: CRA My Account (Fastest)

[Sign in to your CRA account](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/cra-login-services.html) (My Account) and open your RRSP details. Your current RRSP deduction limit will be displayed there, along with any unused contributions you've already made but haven't deducted yet.

If you don't have an account, you'll need to register. You can sign in with a CRA user ID and password, or through a Sign-In Partner, which lets you use your online banking credentials -- most major Canadian banks take part. To verify your identity when you register, CRA can mail a security code to your address on file (it takes up to 10 business days), or you can use its document verification service with government photo ID and your phone's camera to get access right away.

**One thing to know:** CRA's online figures can be delayed, especially early in the year. Your financial institution may not have reported your recent contributions yet. If you make contributions in January or February 2027 for the 2026 tax year (the deadline is March 1, 2027), they might not show up in your account until later in the spring.

## Method 2: Notice of Assessment

Your **Notice of Assessment (NOA)** is the letter CRA sends after processing your tax return each year. It shows your assessed income, any balance owing or refund, and -- in the **RRSP Deduction Limit Statement** -- your **RRSP deduction limit for the following year**. The NOA for your 2025 return shows your limit for 2026.

The NOA reflects your room as of when your return was assessed. If your limit changes after that (because of a reassessment, for example), CRA may send you Form T1028 with the updated figure.

You can view past NOAs in your CRA account. They go back several years.

## Method 3: Call CRA

If you can't access your account and don't have your NOA handy, call the CRA individual tax enquiries line at **1-800-959-8281**. Agents are available weekdays from 8 am to 8 pm ET, and after verifying your identity they can tell you your current RRSP deduction limit. The same number also has an automated service with longer hours.

## How RRSP Room Is Calculated

Your annual RRSP room equals **18% of your previous year's earned income**, up to the annual maximum. For 2026, the maximum is **$33,810** (based on your 2025 earned income).

The full formula:

> RRSP Deduction Limit = 18% of prior year earned income (up to annual max) + unused room from previous years − pension adjustment (PA)

Unused room carries forward. If you had $20,000 in room last year and contributed $0, that $20,000 rolls into this year's limit on top of any new room you've earned.

### What Counts as "Earned Income"

Not all income creates RRSP room. Here's what qualifies:

- Employment income (T4 box 14)
- Net self-employment income
- Net rental income
- Alimony or support payments received
- CPP/QPP disability payments
- Research grants

And here's what **does not** count:

- Investment income (interest, dividends, capital gains)
- Pension income
- Employment Insurance benefits
- Workers' compensation
- Old Age Security
- Social assistance payments

This distinction catches people off guard. If you earned $80,000 in salary and $20,000 in dividends in 2025, only the $80,000 generates RRSP room for 2026. Your 18% is calculated on $80,000, giving you $14,400 in new room -- not the $18,000 you might expect from your $100,000 total income.

## The Pension Adjustment

If your employer offers a registered pension plan (defined benefit or defined contribution), your T4 will include a **pension adjustment (PA)** in box 52. This amount reduces your RRSP room for the next year because, from CRA's perspective, you're already saving for retirement through the pension.

A large PA can significantly eat into your RRSP room. Someone with a generous defined benefit pension might find their RRSP room is only a few thousand dollars per year, even on a six-figure salary.

## Why Your Room Might Be Lower Than Expected

If you log in and the number seems wrong, check these common causes:

- **Pension adjustment:** Your workplace pension reduces your room. Look at box 52 on your T4.
- **Unfiled tax returns:** CRA calculates your room based on returns you've actually filed. If you skipped a year, CRA has no earned income to work with for that period. File the missing returns and your room should update.
- **Recent contributions not yet processed:** Contributions made in the last few months may not be reflected yet.
- **Prior-year deductions:** If you contributed in a previous year but chose to defer the deduction, CRA still tracks the contribution. The room is used even if the deduction isn't.

If you've never filed a tax return, your RRSP room could be sitting at $0, regardless of how much you've earned. CRA needs your filed returns to calculate earned income. File those returns -- even late ones -- and the room will appear.
