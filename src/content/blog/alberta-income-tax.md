Alberta is one of the lowest-taxed provinces in Canada. No provincial sales tax, the highest basic personal amount in the country, and an 8% starting income tax rate on the first $61,200 of taxable income. If you earn the same salary in Alberta as you would in Ontario, you keep more of it at every income above about $40,000. (BC's income tax is actually a little lower than Alberta's at most incomes from about $25,000 to $148,000, but BC charges a 7% PST.)

Here is how Alberta's tax system works for the 2026 tax year -- the return you file in spring 2027 -- and what it actually means for your paycheque.

## No Provincial Sales Tax

Alberta is one of the few provinces with no PST. You pay the 5% federal GST on purchases, and that is it. In Ontario, you are paying 13% HST. In BC, you are paying 5% GST plus 7% PST on most goods. In Quebec, it is 5% GST plus 9.975% QST.

This does not show up on your income tax return, but it absolutely affects your cost of living. On a $30,000 car, the difference between 5% GST in Alberta and 13% HST in Ontario is $2,400 out of your pocket. On every major purchase -- furniture, electronics, home renovations -- Albertans pay less in consumption taxes than residents of provinces with a PST or HST.

## The Highest Basic Personal Amount in Canada

Alberta's basic personal amount for 2026 is **$22,769**. That is the amount of income you can earn before you owe a single dollar of provincial income tax.

Compare that to other provinces:

- **Federal BPA:** $16,452
- **Ontario BPA:** $12,989
- **BC BPA:** $13,216
- **Alberta BPA:** $22,769

The gap is significant. An Albertan earning $22,000 pays zero provincial income tax. An Ontarian earning the same amount has roughly $9,000 of income above Ontario's basic personal amount; Ontario's low-income tax reduction and LIFT credit cancel the income tax on it, but they still owe a $109 Ontario Health Premium.

## Alberta's Provincial Tax Brackets

Alberta uses a progressive bracket system. In 2025 it added a new 8% bracket on the first $60,000 of taxable income; for 2026 that bracket, like the others, is indexed up by 2% and now runs to $61,200.

| Taxable Income | Alberta Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to $61,200 | 8% |
| $61,200 to $154,259 | 10% |
| $154,259 to $185,111 | 12% |
| $185,111 to $246,813 | 13% |
| $246,813 to $370,220 | 14% |
| Over $370,220 | 15% |

The long 10% bracket, which runs all the way to $154,259, is the real story. If you earn $100,000, your Alberta tax is 8% on the first $61,200 and 10% on the rest (minus the basic personal amount credit). In Ontario, you would be in the second provincial bracket at 9.15%, with the 11.16% bracket starting at $107,785.

The top combined marginal rate (federal plus Alberta) is 48%, on taxable income over $370,220. That is significantly lower than Ontario's top combined rate of about 53.53% or Quebec's, which exceeds 53%.

## No Health Premium

Ontario charges a health premium of up to $900 per year on taxable income above $20,000. It is calculated on your tax return and added to your provincial tax owing.

Alberta does not have this. Health care is funded through general provincial revenue. One more line item you do not have to think about.

## CPP and EI Are the Same

Alberta uses the same Canada Pension Plan and Employment Insurance as every province except Quebec. For 2026, the CPP employee rate is 5.95% on earnings between $3,500 and $74,600, with CPP2 at 4% on earnings between $74,600 and $85,000. The EI employee rate is 1.63% on insurable earnings up to $68,900.

These payroll deductions are identical whether you live in Calgary, Toronto, or Vancouver. The only province with different rules is Quebec, which runs its own QPP and QPIP programs.

## The Trade-Offs

Alberta's low tax environment is real, but it is not free money. The province funds services differently -- relying more heavily on natural resource royalties, which can be volatile. When oil prices drop, provincial budgets tighten and spending debates get heated.

There is also no PST credit or rebate program for low-income Albertans the way some provinces offer sales tax credits. The GST/HST credit from the federal government still applies to all Canadians, but the provincial layer of support is thinner.

And of course, income tax is only one factor in your financial picture. Housing costs, job market, and quality of life vary widely across the country. A person earning $80,000 in Calgary may come out ahead on taxes but face different trade-offs than someone earning the same in Halifax or Winnipeg.

## Filing Your Alberta Taxes

Alberta residents file a single income tax return with the CRA, just like residents of every province except Quebec. Your provincial tax is calculated on the same return -- you do not need to file anything separately with the Alberta government.

Most tax software handles this automatically. You enter your income, the software applies both federal and Alberta brackets, and you see your refund or balance owing.

The filing deadline for the 2026 tax year is **April 30, 2027**. Self-employed individuals have until June 15, 2027 to file, but any balance owing is still due by April 30, 2027.
